# Results · H19

## Main results
- **MTG-vulnerable Sst supertypes:** Sst_2, Sst_23, Sst_20, Sst_3, Sst_22, Sst_11 and Sst_25. They make up about 5–7% of GABAergic neurons.
- **Out-of-region test** (ρ with local CPS):

| Region | Vulnerable Sst set | Whole Sst subclass | L2/3 IT |
|---|---|---|---|
| MTG (discovery, n = 83) | −0.38 (p < 0.001) | −0.23 (p = 0.04) | −0.42 (p < 0.001) |
| **AnG (n = 43)** | **−0.29 (p = 0.06)**; Sst_23 −0.45, Sst_20 −0.34 | −0.11 (n.s.) | **−0.46 (p = 0.002)** |
| V1C (n = 43) | −0.08 (n.s.) | +0.19 (n.s.) | −0.25 (n.s.) |

- **Tissue losses in AnG (high vs Not AD/Low):**
    - excitatory drive: 0.12 [0.03, 0.21];
    - fraction of Sst neurons lost: 0.15 [−0.01, 0.28].
- **Bridge** (χ² over 6 features; offset only = 24.5):

| Tissue input | χ² median [95%] | Right signs | beta pred / obs | slope pred / obs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| L2/3 IT only (H16) | 12.3 [5.6, 21.3] | 100% | −0.19 / −0.35 | +0.24 / +0.68 |
| vulnerable Sst only | 8.1 [3.4, 24.5] | 96% | −0.23 / −0.35 | +0.41 / +0.68 |

## Consequences
- **H15's "SST stable posteriorly" was wrong at the resolution that matters.** The subclass share hid a decline of specific superficial Sst supertypes, which carries over from MTG to AnG (marginal at n = 43). The literature check was right on this point. V1C shows neither loss clearly.
- **With both superficial losses sized from AnG tissue, the circuit reproduces the EEG phenotype almost to size.**
    - beta: 78% of the observed effect;
    - slope: 90%;
    - every 24–47 Hz band within its SE;
    - all signs right, and χ² 4.1 on 6 features with nothing fitted to EEG.
- **H\* is revised:** the posterior lesion is a **superficial-layer** loss, L2/3 IT pyramids plus superficial Sst supertypes, not excitatory loss alone. Gabitto 2024 places the vulnerable Sst supertypes in superficial layers.
- **Limits:**
    - shares, not absolute counts;
    - the AnG Sst result is marginal;
    - the bump is still under-predicted (0.014 vs 0.035);
    - different people from the EEG cohort;
    - one circuit family.
